Chapter I

Rarities

Every card carries a rarity symbol at the bottom center, below the text box.[8] Common to Legendary climb from a circle through a book, triangle, diamond and pentagon. The three rarest are rainbow foil.

About these numbersRavensburger doesn’t publish pull rates.[3] The rates here are community estimates from opened boxes and cases,[4] written as cards to packs: 1:96 means about one card in every 96 packs. Values are typical prices for single, ungraded, near-mint cards in late 2026.[5][6][7] Popular cards sell well above their range, and foil versions of lower rarities usually cost a little more.

Chapter II

Finishes

Finish and rarity are separate. A foil Legendary is still a Legendary: the symbol sets the rarity, the finish is how the card is printed.[9] Common to Legendary each come plain or foil; Epic, Enchanted and Iconic each have a foil all their own.

Raised or textured cardsNo finish is officially called embossed; read the rarity symbol. A card that looks like a base card but has a raised, glossy texture on the character is a Legendary from Set 5 on, and with foil as well it’s a foil Legendary. Raised 3D stamping with the rainbow iris symbol is Iconic.

Outside booster packsPromo cards from events and Organized Play kits, oversized cards in some gift sets and Illumineer’s Quest scenario cards come in their own finishes. They aren’t part of the rarity ladder.[11]

Chapter III

What’s in a pack

A booster pack holds twelve cards, and one of them is always foil.[3][4]

Slots 1–6
Commons.
Slots 7–9
Uncommons.
Slots 10–11
Rare or better: usually Rares, sometimes a Super Rare, Legendary or Epic.
Slot 12
The foil. Any rarity can turn up here, and it’s where Enchanted and Iconic cards appear.

A booster box holds 24 packs.
